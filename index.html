About

From a data-mining elective to three first-author papers.

I build reinforcement learning agents that stay fair when resources are scarce and adapt when the world doesn't hold still.

It started with a data-mining elective at DA-IICT — cricket-score forecasting, followed by a disease-prediction project I kept extending long after the course ended. A reinforcement learning course the next semester, taught by Prof. Abhishek Jindal, turned that curiosity into something sharper: systems that don't just predict, but make decisions and improve through experience. That interest grew into a research internship, my B.Tech. major project, and three first-author research efforts:

  • Soft Actor-Critic for joint precoding and phase-shift optimization in RIS-aided MIMO — IEEE VTC2025-Fall (published).
  • Max-min fairness in wireless resource allocation — optimization objectives that keep the worst-served user from being left behind (IEEE TMLCN, major revision).
  • Critic-free GRPO for phase optimization under imperfect hardware — policies that adapt to practical hardware constraints (manuscript in preparation).

My research interests have since expanded beyond wireless communication into other domains where reliable decision-making is challenging. In robotic manipulation, I've investigated gradient-explosion failure modes in critic-free reinforcement learning — studying how optimization instability affects an agent's ability to learn useful behaviours. I've also worked on a clinical decision-support agent designed to recognize uncertainty and know when to abstain rather than answer.

Across wireless systems, robotics, and healthcare, I'm interested in how policy optimization, reward design, and uncertainty shape what an agent can learn, how it adapts, and how reliably it makes decisions.

Outside research, I'm a full-stack developer at Oracle, working on Fusion Recruiting Cloud. Building production software keeps me grounded in a different but equally important question: what does it take to make a system work reliably beyond a controlled experiment?

I'm currently applying to graduate programs in Computer Science, Data Science, and AI/RL for Fall 2027, looking to deepen my research in reinforcement learning, adaptive decision-making, and intelligent systems.

Always happy to talk about reinforcement learning, meta-learning, robotics, optimization, or the places where these ideas break in the wild. Feel free to get in touch.

Rate optimization in distributed RIS-aided MIMO with Soft Actor-Critic

IEEE VTC2025-Fall · published
The gapJointly designing beamforming and phase shifts across several distributed surfaces is non-convex, with unit-modulus constraints coupling the surfaces. Iterative solvers have to re-run for every channel realization.
My contributionA continuous-control MDP solved with Soft Actor-Critic. A single learned policy outputs the whole configuration in one forward pass, and entropy regularization keeps it exploring a multi-modal phase landscape.

Max-min fairness in stacked intelligent metasurfaces via deep RL

IEEE TMLCN · minor revision submitted
The gapExisting max-min designs for SIM-assisted downlinks use alternating optimization (geometric programming plus gradient descent-ascent), which must be re-solved for every channel realization. Prior DRL work on SIM optimized sum-rate, which can starve the weakest users.
My contributionA deep RL formulation for the non-smooth max-min objective, where only the worst-off user drives the reward. It jointly learns transmit power and wave-domain phase shifts across every stacked layer, and it is objective-agnostic, so it carries over to other fairness-constrained allocation problems.

Meta-RL with GRPO for fast adaptation to changing channels

in preparation · targeting IEEE TVT / TMLCN
The gapA DRL policy trained for one set of channel conditions degrades when those conditions drift, and retraining from scratch is impractical in sequential, non-stationary environments like a real wireless link.
My contributionA meta-RL framework built on Group Relative Policy Optimization. It is critic-free and uses group-normalized advantages, so a meta-learned policy adapts to a new channel regime in a few updates without waiting for a value function to catch up.

Finding and fixing GRPO's collapse in robot manipulation

independent research · in progress
The gapCritic-free RL methods like GRPO promise cheaper robot-policy fine-tuning — no value network to train or store. Every critic-free method I tested collapsed to a permanent 0% success rate within a few dozen iterations, and the standard fix the method's own recipe specifies was never actually wired into the training loss.
My contributionTraced the collapse to a specific mechanism — a gradient-norm blowup under group-std normalization on near-degenerate rollout groups — and fixed it by correctly implementing a KL-to-reference penalty. Combined with finding the real bottleneck was the behavior-cloning data, not the RL algorithm, this took a critic-free policy from 10% to 70% deterministic success on Meta-World pick-and-place.
